Rules of the Contest “Digital Mine on OpenEgiz”

1. General Provisions

1.1. These Rules set out the procedure for holding the open contest “Digital Mine on OpenEgiz” (the “contest”). The subject of the contest is the creation of a digital twin of a mine on the OpenEgiz platform.

1.2. The organizer of the contest is the DigitalEgiz lab (the “Organizer”). The Organizer approves the Rules, publishes Official Clarifications and pays the prize. The venue for the contest is provided by al-Farabi Kazakh National University (KazNU).

1.3. Places in the contest are determined by the AI Jury in accordance with the Rubric and the AI Jury pipeline fixed before the Freeze (clause 8.5). The Organizer and any other persons take no part in determining places.

1.4. The terms of the contest are established by these Rules and by Official Clarifications. The Rules are published in English, Kazakh and Russian; in the event of any discrepancy between the texts, the Russian text shall prevail.

1.5. Official resources of the contest:

  • contest website — openegiz-contest.github.io;
  • contest organization on GitHub — openegiz-contest;
  • contest GitHub Discussions — questions and Official Clarifications;
  • Instagram digital_twin_kaznu — announcements.

1.6. All dates and times are given in Astana time (UTC+5).

2. Terms

  • AI Jury — the Judges taken together.
  • Appeal — a Team's claim of a factual error in a Build Report or in a finding on an attempt to influence the AI Jury (section 12).
  • Build Report — the report compiled by the Builder on running a Submission: the results of executing the instructions, whether the declared functions work, and logs.
  • Builder — an AI agent that runs a Submission following the README instructions and compiles the Build Report. The Builder does not award points.
  • Example Mine — the demonstration digital mine included in OpenEgiz (examples/mine), which shows how a digital mine is structured on the platform. The Example Mine is not a Submission and is not a mandatory basis for one.
  • Final — the concluding stage of the contest, broadcast live, at which the Judges determine first to third places.
  • Finalists — the Teams whose Submissions have advanced to the Final (clause 10.1).
  • Freeze — the moment at which acceptance of Submissions ends, after which Team Repositories are read-only (clause 7.1).
  • Judge — an AI model that operates within an agent and awards points according to the Rubric independently of the other Judges.
  • Judge Environment — the reference computing environment in which every Submission is run. Its specifications are published on the contest website.
  • Mine Map — the list of digital mine Modules published on the contest website.
  • Module — a functional area of a digital mine (for example, haulage and dispatch, ventilation).
  • Official Clarification — an answer by the Organizer in the contest GitHub Discussions that has the same force as the Rules.
  • OpenEgiz — an open platform for digital twins (github.com/aleka07/openegiz).
  • Organizer — the DigitalEgiz lab.
  • Participant — an individual registered as a member of a Team.
  • Qualifying Round — the evaluation of all Submissions by all Judges that precedes the Final.
  • Registration — the filing of a Team's application through the form on the contest website (section 5).
  • Rubric — the criteria and weights according to which the Judges award points (section 9).
  • Runners-up — the Teams that have taken second and third places.
  • Stand — an instance of OpenEgiz with a Submission, running in the Judge Environment.
  • Submission — the contents of the main branch of a Team Repository at the moment of the Freeze.
  • Team — a group of one to four Participants presenting one Submission.
  • Team Repository — a Team's private repository in the openegiz-contest organization on GitHub, created from the Template.
  • Template — a complete copy of the OpenEgiz release fixed for the contest, from which Team Repositories are created.
  • Tie-break Rule — the procedure for awarding a place when there is no majority of the Judges' votes (clause 10.4).
  • Winner — the Team that has taken first place.

3. Prize

3.1. The Winner is awarded a prize of 1,000,000 (one million) tenge.

3.2. If the Winner consists of more than one Participant, the prize is divided equally among them. The shares are received by the Participants who are members of the Team at the moment Registration closes. Participants confirm their agreement to this procedure at Registration.

3.3. The prize is paid to each Participant in the amount of their share, in tenge, in cash or by transfer to the Participant's personal bank account.

3.4. The Runners-up are awarded diplomas and receive an offer of an internship or employment at the DigitalEgiz lab; their Submissions are published on the OpenEgiz website. No cash prize is paid to the Runners-up.

3.5. The prize is not awarded if no Submission scores more than 40 points (clause 9.3).

3.6. Taxes, duties, bank fees and other expenses related to receiving the prize are borne by the Participant personally, in accordance with the laws of the country of their tax residence. If the laws of the Republic of Kazakhstan require the Organizer to withhold tax when paying the prize, the prize is paid net of the amount withheld.

4. Participants

4.1. Any individual may become a Participant in the contest.

4.2. A Participant may be a member of only one Team.

4.3. Any tools, including artificial intelligence tools, may be used to create a Submission.

5. Registration

5.1. A Team registers through the form posted on the contest website. The form requires the Team name and, for each Participant, their full name, place of study or work, email address and GitHub username.

5.2. At Registration, each Participant confirms:

  • agreement with these Rules;
  • consent to the distribution of the Submission under the MIT license (clause 6.6);
  • agreement with the procedure for dividing the prize (clause 3.2);
  • consent to the processing of personal data (section 15).

5.3. After Registration, a Team Repository openegiz-contest/team-NNN, where NNN is the sequential number of the Registration, is created for the Team, and GitHub invitations are sent to the Participants. Participants receive write access to the Team Repository without the right to change its settings or to delete it.

5.4. Until Registration closes, the membership of a Team is changed using the “Edit response” function of the registration form. If the same GitHub account or email address is listed as a member of two Teams, the person is included in the Team that registered earlier.

5.5. Registration closes at the moment of the Freeze. After Registration closes, the membership of Teams does not change.

6. Task and Submission

6.1. The contest task is to create the Team's own digital mine on the OpenEgiz platform. The digital mine may represent any part of a mining enterprise, including an open pit, an underground mine or a processing plant. There are no tracks or categories. The Team determines its set of Modules at its own discretion, guided by the Mine Map; Modules not included in the Mine Map are evaluated on an equal footing with those included. The depth of development of Modules takes priority over their number.

6.2. The Submission must be built on OpenEgiz. This means that:

  • mine objects are represented by twins in Eclipse Ditto;
  • data is transmitted through the platform (MQTT → Ditto);
  • the user interface obtains data from the platform.

An application that operates separately from the platform and is connected to it only formally is not considered to be built on OpenEgiz (clause 9.3).

6.3. Work is carried out in the Team Repository. Changes to any parts, including the platform core, are permitted. The Example Mine demonstrates how a digital mine is structured on the platform; its use is not mandatory. Only what has been created by the Team is evaluated (clause 9.1).

6.4. The Submission must start in the Judge Environment by following the README.md instructions without manual intervention. The Builder follows the instructions literally; a Submission that has not been started by following the instructions is deemed to have failed to start. An instruction step that ends in an error is repeated once without changes; the result of the last attempt counts. The specifications of the Judge Environment, including the start-up time limit, are published on the contest website. The Judge Environment contains no access keys, paid services or accounts of the Organizer.

6.5. The Submission must contain in the repository root:

  • README.md — instructions for installing and running the Submission from scratch;
  • SUBMISSION.md — a description of the Submission in the form provided in the Template: Team members, implemented Modules, their working status and limitations, justification of the Modules, a “Data and Assumptions” section, and a pointer to the visualization;
  • presentation.pdf — a presentation of the Submission.

A video may be provided at the Team's discretion as a link in SUBMISSION.md.

6.6. The Submission is distributed under the MIT license. Copyright belongs to the Team; the Team is entitled to name itself in the LICENSE file. The OpenEgiz code included in the Template is distributed under its own licenses.

6.7. Submissions are accepted in Kazakh, Russian or English.

Data

6.8. Synthetic data and simulation may be used, provided that the parameters are justified.

6.9. Open datasets may be used. A dataset placed in the Team Repository must have a license that permits the distribution of copies. The Team is responsible for the availability of a dataset downloaded from an external source following the README.md instructions; if the dataset is not downloaded, the Submission is deemed to have failed to start. The license and source of a dataset are stated in README.md or next to the data.

6.10. The use of confidential data of organizations and of personal data is prohibited. The Team is responsible for the lawfulness of its use of data.

6.11. SUBMISSION.md must contain a “Data and Assumptions” section stating which data are synthetic and which are real, the sources of parameters (equipment data sheets, reference literature, standards, datasets) and the licenses of the datasets.

6.12. The justification of a Module follows the structure “problem → metric → result”. The result is measured on the Stand by comparison with the same mine without that Module (for example, throughput in tonnes per shift without dispatch and with it). Only effects reproducible on the Stand are taken into account; claims not confirmed on the Stand are not evaluated.

7.1. At the moment of the Freeze, the Participants' write access is revoked and the last commit of the main branch of each Team Repository is recorded, after which the repositories are made public and read-only. The recorded commit is deemed to be the Submission. Other branches, pull requests and changes made after the Freeze are not considered. The list of recorded commits is published.

7.2. A Submission whose main branch does not differ from the Template is deemed not to have been submitted.

8. AI Jury

8.1. The composition of the AI Jury is published before the Freeze (clause 8.5).

8.2. A Submission is evaluated in two stages:

  1. The Builder runs the Submission following the README.md instructions in the isolated Judge Environment and compiles the Build Report.
  2. The Judges, independently of each other, award points according to the Rubric on the basis of the Build Report, the source code, SUBMISSION.md, the presentation and the user interface of the Stand.

8.3. In the Qualifying Round, each Submission is evaluated by all Judges. Build Reports, the Judges' scores with their reasoning, and work logs are published.

8.4. If technical limitations of AI services prevent all Judges from evaluating all Submissions in time, some of the Judges evaluate only the Submissions at the top of the ranking compiled from the scores of the other Judges. In that case, the score of a Submission is the average of the scores of the Judges who evaluated it. The date of the Final is not postponed.

8.5. The Rubric forms part of these Rules. The AI Jury pipeline (the Judges' prompts and the program code) is fixed before the Freeze. Before the Freeze, the composition of the AI Jury and a checksum (hash) of the fixed version of the pipeline are published; after the Qualifying Round results are published, the Judges' prompts and the program code of the pipeline are published, against which the checksum can be verified. Once fixed, the pipeline is not changed, except for corrections of errors that do not affect evaluation. A notice stating the reasons and the new checksum is published for each such correction.

9. Rubric

9.1. Each Judge scores a Submission on a scale from 0 to 100 points according to the following criteria. For each criterion the Judge determines a level from 0 to 4; the score for the criterion equals the criterion's maximum points multiplied by the level and divided by 4. Only what has been created by the Team and confirmed by the Judge on the Stand or in the source code is taken into account; statements in SUBMISSION.md and in the presentation that are not confirmed on the Stand earn no points. The Example Mine and its parts used without modification earn no points.

Criterion Points Description
Production Fidelity 15 The model on the Stand corresponds to real mining operations: quantities are plausible and consistent with each other, processes are interlinked, and real constraints are taken into account
Module Effect 40 The benefit of the Modules is proven on the Stand by comparison with the same mine without the Module (clause 6.12): the comparison is fair and the effect is reproducible. The soundness of the result is evaluated, not the size of the effect
Mine Visualization 15 On the Stand, the mine is presented as a spatial object showing the data of the twins, the course of the operations and the effects of the Modules, and not only as charts and tables. The technology is not restricted
Mine Map Coverage 15 The number of Modules working on the Stand. A Module counts if it changes the operation of the mine or produces a decision that does not exist without it
Presentation 15 presentation.pdf clearly sets out the work done and the measured result; its content matches the Stand

9.2. The score of a Submission in the Qualifying Round is the arithmetic mean of the Judges' scores.

9.3. The score of a Submission that has failed to start by following README.md or is not built on OpenEgiz (clause 6.2) may not exceed 40 points. The Team of such a Submission may not become the Winner.

10. Final and Determination of Places

10.1. The 10 Submissions with the highest Qualifying Round scores advance to the Final. If there are fewer than ten Submissions, all Submissions advance to the Final.

10.2. The Final is held live on YouTube. The Judges discuss the Finalists' Submissions and determine first to third places. The discussion logs are published.

10.3. Places are awarded in order, starting with first place. A place is awarded to the Finalist for whom more than half of the Judges have voted. If there is no majority, a further round of discussion is held. The maximum number of rounds is published together with the AI Jury pipeline.

10.4. Tie-break Rule. If no majority is reached after the last round, the place is awarded to the Finalist with the highest Qualifying Round score among those that have not yet received a place. If the scores are equal, the place is awarded to the Finalist with the highest average score for the “Module Effect” criterion and, if they are still equal, to the Team that registered earlier.

10.5. If a Judge is unavailable during the Final, the decision is taken by a majority vote of the remaining Judges; if the votes are tied, the Tie-break Rule applies.

11. Disqualification

11.1. A Submission is disqualified for an attempt to influence the AI Jury, that is, placing hidden or explicit instructions to the Builder and the Judges in the instructions, code, data, presentation or interface (prompt injection).

11.2. The presence of such instructions is determined by a separate AI agent. Its finding is published together with the Qualifying Round reports and may be appealed under the procedure set out in section 12.

12. Appeals

12.1. A Team is entitled to file an Appeal within 48 hours after the publication of the Qualifying Round reports. The procedure for filing is announced at the same time as the reports are published.

12.2. An Appeal is accepted only in respect of a factual error in a Build Report or in a finding on an attempt to influence the AI Jury — for example, if the Build Report states that a Module does not start and the Team presents a log confirming that it starts. Disagreement with the points awarded is not grounds for an Appeal.

12.3. Appeals are reviewed automatically: the Submission is run again, and the dispute is resolved by a separate Judge. If an Appeal is upheld, the Judges re-evaluate the Submission on the basis of the corrected Build Report; the final score cannot be lowered as a result. The outcome of the review is published and is final.

13. Winner

13.1. The Winner confirms participation within 72 hours after the results are announced, by an email to developerbeck@digitalegiz.kz or a message in the contest GitHub Discussions from any Participant of the Team. If no confirmation is received, the Team next in the final ranking of the Final is recognized as the Winner.

13.2. A talk by the Winner is a condition for receiving the prize. The talk does not affect the allocation of places and is given in person at al-Farabi Kazakh National University or online, at the Winner's choice.

14. Questions and Amendments to the Rules

14.1. Questions about the contest are asked in the contest GitHub Discussions. The Organizer's answers in GitHub Discussions are Official Clarifications and have the same force as the Rules. Answers given in other channels are not official.

14.2. After the start of the contest, the Rubric and the Tie-break Rule shall not be changed; the composition of the AI Jury shall not be changed after its publication. The contest dates may be changed by an Official Clarification published in advance.

15. Personal Data

15.1. Participants' personal data (full name, place of study or work, email address) are used solely for running the contest and paying the prize, and are stored by the Organizer with restricted access.

15.2. Only the Team name, the Participants' GitHub usernames and, after the Freeze, the contents of the Submission are published.
