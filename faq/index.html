Questions and Answers

These answers are provided for convenience and do not replace the Rules; in case of any discrepancy, the Rules prevail. Questions not answered here can be asked in GitHub Discussions.

Can I register or change a Submission?

No. Registration is closed and Submissions were frozen on 25 October 2026 at 23:59 Astana time; Team Repositories are read-only. Rules, clause 7.1.

Participation

Who ran the contest?

The Organizer is Gulshat Amanzholovna Amirkhanova; she held the contest in her own name and pays the prize from her personal funds. The contest was held with the support of the DigitalEgiz lab of al-Farabi Kazakh National University (KazNU). Rules, clause 1.2.

Who could take part?

Any individual; a person under 18 took part with the consent of their legal representative. Rules, clause 4.1.

Could a person take part alone?

Yes. A Team could have one to four Participants. Rules, section 2.

Submission

What did Teams have to build?

Their own digital mine on the OpenEgiz platform. Each Team chose its Modules itself, using the Mine Map as a guide; Modules not on the Map were evaluated on an equal footing. Depth of development matters more than the number of Modules. Rules, clause 6.1.

What is the Concept?

The first section of SUBMISSION.md, written by the Team before the code: which enterprise or which part of it the Submission represents, who uses the twin, which decision it helps to make and why this choice was made. The object could be real or fictional; for a fictional object, the Concept states what it resembles and where the numbers come from. The Concept was scored under the "Concept and Presentation" criterion; a real object with sourced numbers scored higher. Rules, clauses 6.13 and 9.1.

What is the Default Solution?

The Submission that AI agents build from the Template given one sentence, with no Concept and no edits; the reference point under the Rules. The Default Solution has not been published; the Judges assigned levels using the level descriptions of the Rubric. Rules, clause 9.4.

Did Teams have to build on the Example Mine?

No. The Example Mine shows how a digital mine is structured on the platform and is not the basis for a Submission. Only what the Team created was evaluated; the unchanged Example Mine earned no points. Rules, clauses 6.3 and 9.1.

What does "built on OpenEgiz" mean?

Mine objects are represented by twins in Eclipse Ditto, data flows through the platform (MQTT → Ditto), and the user interface gets its data from the platform. A Submission not built on OpenEgiz received no more than 40 points and could not become the Winner. Rules, clauses 6.2 and 9.3.

Could Teams modify the platform core?

Yes, any part could be modified. Rules, clause 6.3.

Could Teams use AI during development?

Yes, any tools could be used. Rules, clause 4.3.

What does a Submission include?

In the root of the main branch: README.md — instructions for installing and running from scratch; SUBMISSION.md — a description of the Submission in the form provided in the Template, starting with the Concept; presentation.pdf — the presentation. A video is optional, as a link in SUBMISSION.md. Rules, clause 6.5.

Was 3D visualization required?

No. The mine had to be shown as a spatial object: a 2D map with moving equipment, an animated diagram or a 3D scene. The technology was not restricted. Rules, clause 9.1.

In what language were Submissions written?

Kazakh, Russian or English. Rules, clause 6.7.

Could Teams use real data?

Yes, open datasets with a license that permits the distribution of copies. Confidential data of organizations and personal data were prohibited. Checked datasets are listed on the Data page. Rules, clauses 6.8–6.11.

Running and Evaluation

Does OpenEgiz run on Windows and macOS?

Yes. OpenEgiz runs on Linux, macOS and Windows (via WSL2); instructions are in OPENEGIZ.md in the Team Repository. Evaluation took place in the Judge Environment, Ubuntu 24.04 amd64 with Docker.

What happened if a Submission did not start?

An instruction step that failed was repeated once without changes. A Submission that did not start by following README.md received no more than 40 points and could not become the Winner. Rules, clauses 6.4 and 9.3.

Did the instructions have to be a single command?

No. The instructions could contain several commands; they were run in order and literally, without manual intervention. Rules, clause 6.4.

Who evaluated the Submissions?

The AI Jury: AI models working under the Rubric and the pipeline whose checksum was published on 27 October 2026, after the Freeze (Discussion #3). The Rubric is part of the Rules; the Judges' prompts and the pipeline code and the reports and scores for each Submission were published after the Qualifying Round. Rules, clauses 1.3 and 8.5.

Could a score be appealed?

An Appeal could be filed within 48 hours after the reports were published, and only regarding a factual error in the Build Report or a finding on an attempt to influence the AI Jury. Disagreement with the scores was not grounds for an Appeal; an upheld Appeal could not lower a score. 43 Appeals were decided: 38 upheld, 5 rejected; the outcome of each is published. Rules, section 12.

What counted as an attempt to influence the AI Jury?

Placing in the instructions, code, data, presentation or interface hidden or explicit instructions addressed to the Builder or the Judges and aimed at changing their work or scores (prompt injection). Such a Submission was disqualified. Prompts of the Submission's functions addressed to its own AI models did not count. Rules, section 11.

Prize

How is the prize distributed?

The prize of 1,000,000 tenge is awarded only to the Winner. If the Team has several Participants, the prize is divided equally among the Participants who were members of the Team when Registration closed. The prize is paid without tax withholding; each Participant pays the tax on their share themselves. Rules, clauses 3.1–3.3 and 3.6.

What do the Runners-up receive?

Diplomas and an offer of an internship or a job at the DigitalEgiz lab; the Submissions of the Runners-up are published on the Submissions page. No cash prize is paid. Rules, clause 3.4.

What are the Winner's obligations?

To confirm participation within 72 hours after the results are announced and, no later than 30 November 2026, to give a talk in person at al-Farabi Kazakh National University (KazNU) or online. The talk is a condition for receiving the prize and does not affect the allocation of places. The prize is paid no later than 30 days after the talk. Rules, section 13.
