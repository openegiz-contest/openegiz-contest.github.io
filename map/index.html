Mine Map

The Mine Map is a list of Modules that a digital mine may consist of. There are no tracks or categories: the Team decides which Modules to build and how deeply to develop them. Mine Map Coverage is one of the Rubric criteria (15 points); depth of Module development takes priority over the number of Modules. Mine visualization and dashboards do not count as Modules: visualization is assessed under a separate Rubric criterion.

The Example Mine shows how a digital mine is built on the platform: an excavator, two haul trucks on a single 3.2 km road, and a crusher. The Example Mine is not the basis of a Submission: the Team creates its own mine, with its own mine type, equipment and processes. Only what the Team has created is assessed.

A Module in OpenEgiz

A Submission must be built on OpenEgiz (Rules, clause 6.2). A Module is implemented with the platform's tools:

  • Twins are things in Eclipse Ditto. The mine hierarchy is set by the _parents attribute and displayed by the OpenEgiz app in Grafana.
  • Telemetry is Ditto protocol messages over MQTT (telemetry/<twin>). Ditto updates the twin's state; Telegraf stores the history in InfluxDB.
  • Dashboards are Grafana with InfluxDB and Ditto data sources.
  • 3D visualization is the Team's choice: Unity WebGL in a Grafana panel, or another technology displayed on the Stand.
  • Application logic (dispatching, models, optimization) is the Team's own service in the compose setup, exchanging data via MQTT or the Ditto API.

Twins and telemetry are described in the OpenEgiz README.

Module Descriptions

Each Module is described by its purpose in real-world mining. The Team chooses the Module's implementation, metrics and data. The justification for each Module is given in SUBMISSION.md following the "problem → metric → result" scheme, with measurement on the Stand (Rules, clause 6.12). Open datasets with suitable licences are listed on the "Data" page; synthetic data with justified parameters is allowed.

1. Haulage and Dispatch

At the mine. The haul truck cycle: loading at the excavator, loaded haul, dumping (crusher, stockpile, waste dump), empty return. Bottlenecks are queues at the excavators and at the dumping points. Dispatching assigns trucks to excavators.

2. Geology and Ore Quality

At the mine. A block model represents the deposit as blocks with a grade of the valuable component. Grade control ensures that ore goes to the processing plant and waste rock goes to the dump; blending keeps the quality of the plant feed within set limits.

3. Drilling and Blasting

At the mine. Holes are drilled in a pattern, charged and blasted. Blast quality, i.e. the size of the broken rock (fragmentation), determines the loading rate and the load on the crusher. Drill rigs record measurement-while-drilling (MWD) data that reflects rock hardness.

4. Ventilation and Safety

At the mine. In underground mines: ventilation by fans and monitoring of gases (methane, carbon monoxide); in open pits: dust and gases after blasting, and slope stability. This area also covers personnel location tracking and vehicle collision avoidance.

5. Energy

At the mine. Electricity is consumed by crushers, mills, fans and electric excavators; haul trucks burn diesel fuel. Losses come from load peaks and equipment running idle.

6. Processing: Crushing, Grinding, Flotation

At the mine. Ore is crushed, ground in mills, and then flotation separates the concentrate. The key indicators are recovery of the valuable component and concentrate grade.

7. Equipment Maintenance and Reliability

At the mine. Planned maintenance and condition-based repairs reduce equipment downtime. The main indicator is mechanical availability.

Own Modules

The Mine Map is not a closed list. A Module not included in the Map (for example, mine dewatering or a fuel and lubricants depot) counts towards coverage on an equal footing with Map Modules; its justification follows the same "problem → metric → result" scheme.
